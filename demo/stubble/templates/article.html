Razor burn or razor bumps? How to tell, and why the neck suffers most

Two different problems with two different fixes, plus the habits that prevent both.

By The Journal Team · October 4, 2026 · 4 minutes

Both are irritation after a shave, and people mix them up. Razor burn shows up within minutes as redness and stinging. Razor bumps show up a day or two later as small raised spots where hair has curled back into skin.

The short version.

  • Burn is the blade scraping skin. Bumps are hair growing inward.
  • The neck suffers most because hair there grows in several directions.
  • Prep, light pressure and a fresh cartridge reduce both.

How to fix burn.

Rinse with cool water, skip the aftershave that contains alcohol, and use a soothing balm. Give the area a day before shaving again.

“If it stings, the blade is probably dull or you pressed too hard.”

How to prevent bumps.

  1. Soften hair with warm water and a rich gel for a full minute.
  2. Shave in the direction the hair grows on your neck.
  3. Exfoliate gently twice a week so hairs can leave the follicle.

If bumps keep coming back, a dermatologist can suggest options, and a single-blade handle may suit curly hair better.
